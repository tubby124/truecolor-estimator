03 / keep the personality

The reusable shop voice

Sound like the person across the counter.

Start with something the customer is actually trying to do: an event, a sign, a message people need to see. Use “you” and “we,” contractions and everyday print language. Be warm without pretending to know their story.

Give them something concrete.

Name one real product or useful detail. If a price matters, give its exact scope and exclusions. Say Saskatoon once where it belongs. End with one easy reply: message, size or deadline.

Repeat the personality, vary the post.

Recipe: everyday situation → one useful print detail → one natural invitation. Rotate between helping someone choose, explaining a material and showing cleared real work. Let the subject decide the opening.

Keep the trust.

No invented customers, “another happy client,” made-up shop anecdotes, blanket rush promises or empty quality claims. Avoid “elevate your brand,” “game-changer,” forced humour and piles of hashtags. A synthetic graphic stays labelled as a concept.

SEO/content principles applied: clear local identity, specific product terms, source-backed pricing and useful next steps. This is a voice demonstration, not a claim about rankings.
