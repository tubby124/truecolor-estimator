For the first two scheduled creative slots, inspect both destinations within 15 minutes; subsequently inspect by the next business day while exception monitoring remains active. Assess duplicate absence only within the inspected provider history and matching scope, never as proof across all time. A successful HTTP response or timer tick alone does not satisfy delivery evidence. If public URL access is unavailable, keep public-view verification pending even when a protected provider read succeeds.

Proposed exception rules for the canonical operational monitor:

- Notify once on a new failed/uncertain provider outcome, lost dispatch acknowledgement, broken approval/rights/fact binding, or overdue approved work beyond 15 minutes. Hold affected work; never replay a provider mutation merely because the acknowledgement is missing.
- Notify once if the configured timer/heartbeat is absent for more than five expected tick intervals or ten minutes, whichever is longer, while scheduling should be enabled; also flag due work while publishing is paused. Keep deliberate pauses distinguishable from failures.
- More than one hour late is already a code-enforced return to draft with revoked approval. Pick a new time and obtain approval; do not catch up by flooding the feed. Do not delete receipts or reset `posting` to hide uncertainty.
- Stay quiet while state is unchanged or healthy. Notify again only on meaningful change, recovery or a necessary owner action. A weekly private learning record is separate from alerts; do not repurpose pilot Telegram authorization into new message traffic.
